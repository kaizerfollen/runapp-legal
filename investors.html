Runcell

Walking is the habit.
Territory is the reason.

A working iOS app, our own backend, and a handful of people who walk further than they used to because of it.

The problem we picked

Fitness apps are good at measuring and bad at making anyone come back. A step counter asks you to care about a number that resets every night. Most people stop caring within a month.

We replaced the number with something people already understand without being taught: a place that is yours, and someone who can take it. The walk is unchanged. The reason to take it is not.

What it does

The map is a grid of hexagons roughly 150 metres across. Walk through one and it fills with your colour. A cell belongs to whoever passed through it last, so your street is yours only until a neighbour goes out.

Conflict, not achievement

Losing a cell is a notification, and getting it back is one walk. That loop needs no reminder to fire.

Groups hold ground

Clans colour districts together. Retention through other people is cheaper than retention through streaks.

Local by nature

Territory only matters against people nearby. Growth is dense and neighbourhood-shaped, not thinly global.

Where we are

  • iOS app and Apple Watch companion — built and running on real devices
  • Own backend in Go, PostgreSQL with PostGIS, deployed and serving
  • Server-side anti-cheat: speed, distance-to-cell ratio, submission frequency
  • Clans, chat, feed, moderation tools — reports and blocks, as required for user content
  • Sign in with Apple, account deletion with access revocation
  • A small group of live testers walking daily

Submitting to the App Store now.
